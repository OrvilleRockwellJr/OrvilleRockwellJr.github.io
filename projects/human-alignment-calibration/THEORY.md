# Theory note: what is being tested, and why the test is the right one

## Setup
For an image x, a network outputs a top-1 prediction with confidence C (max softmax probability). Let Y = 1 if the prediction is correct. Let A be an image-level alignment score: how closely the network's evidence map (here, |d logit_true / d x|, max over colour channels) matches the map of regions humans marked as diagnostic (ClickMe), measured by Spearman rank correlation.

Confidence is *calibrated* if P(Y=1 | C=c) = c. Expected calibration error (ECE) estimates E|P(Y=1|C) - C| by binning.

## Question as a statement about conditional distributions
Does A tell us anything about Y that C does not already?

    H0:  Y is independent of A given C        versus        H1:  E[Y | C, A] != E[Y | C]

## Lemma 1 (information in a finer description)
Let F be the sigma-algebra generated by C and G the one generated by (C, A), so F is contained in G. Then

    Var(E[Y|G]) - Var(E[Y|F]) = E[ (E[Y|G] - E[Y|F])^2 ] >= 0,

with equality iff E[Y|G] = E[Y|F] almost surely.

*Proof.* E[Y|F] = E[ E[Y|G] | F ] (tower property), so E[Y|F] is the L2 projection of E[Y|G] onto the F-measurable functions. Pythagoras gives E[(E[Y|G])^2] = E[(E[Y|F])^2] + E[(E[Y|G] - E[Y|F])^2]. Both conditional expectations have the same mean E[Y], so subtracting its square from both sides turns the second moments into variances. Equality in the last term holds iff the difference is zero a.s. ∎

## Consequence
Var(E[Y|.]) is the *resolution* term of the Brier-score decomposition (Murphy, 1973: Brier = reliability - resolution + uncertainty). Lemma 1 says: adding A to C can raise resolution, and it raises it exactly when A shifts the conditional probability of being correct. Under H0, E[Y|C,A] = E[Y|C], so adding A gives nothing, for the Brier score or any other proper score evaluated at the Bayes-optimal predictor. Under H1 the gain is strictly positive. So "does alignment carry information about correctness beyond confidence?" has a clean, falsifiable answer in terms of H0 versus H1.

## From the population statement to a finite-sample test
1. *Within-bin AUROC.* Split images into 10 confidence-quantile bins. Within each bin compute AUROC of A for Y, then average weighted by the number of positive-negative pairs. Under H0 and thin bins this is near 0.5.
2. *Permutation null.* Shuffle A among images in the same bin, recompute, repeat 5,000 times. Shuffling inside a bin keeps the confidence-correctness relationship fixed and breaks only the A-Y link.
3. *Effect size.* Cross-validated AUROC of a logistic model (Y on logit C and A) minus that of (Y on logit C). A bootstrap interval over out-of-fold predictions gives the uncertainty. This also catches dependence the binned statistic can miss.

**Caveat.** Conditional independence given C implies independence given the bin only if A does not vary systematically with C inside a bin. With 10 quantile bins this residual is small but not zero; the logistic effect size is a second check. This is why both are reported.

## Why excess alignment, not raw alignment
ClickMe maps are centre-biased (ImageNet objects tend to sit near the middle). A saliency map that is simply "bright in the middle" scores above zero without using any object evidence. Define excess alignment e_i = rho(model_i, human_i) - mean_j rho(model_i, human_j) for three other random images j. It removes what is explained by the shared spatial prior. Raw alignment is also reported.

## Terms
- **Saliency / evidence map:** per-pixel magnitude of the gradient of the true-class logit.
- **Spearman correlation with ties:** correlation of average ranks; thresholded or flat regions produce ties, and ignoring them biases rank statistics (the same issue as tied-score plateaus in rank-based selection).
- **Human ceiling (0.65753):** inter-rater Spearman of ClickMe maps reported by the original lab; dividing by it puts alignment on a "fraction of human-to-human agreement" scale.
- **Anchoring index g:** probability drop when the top 20% of human-marked pixels are removed, minus the drop when the same area from another image's map is removed. Positive g means the prediction depends on regions humans consider diagnostic.
- **ECE, Brier, AUROC, Holm correction:** standard; Holm controls the family-wise error across the eight models.
