Generative design that tells you when not to trust it.

At a glance

Question

When can a steering predictor be trusted during generative design, and when does gradient guidance beat generate-and-filter?

Approach

Classifier-guided diffusion for molecules and CRISPR guides, a 5-head bootstrap critic ensemble with an uncertainty gate, and a held-out judge trained on different features and different data.

Finding

For molecules the gate decides whether the critic’s shortlist is usable (5% pass the audit without it, 72% with it; unguided filtering 31%). For guides filtering already reaches 92% and the gate did not help.

Limits

Small public datasets, one target (PTPRS), and a judge that is a model, not an experiment. Nothing here is lab-validated. Method and reproduction.

Research prototype, in silico only. Every score is a prediction from small models trained on public data (ChEMBL, B3DB, BBBP, Tox21, ToxCast, and SpCas9 guide-efficacy screens from three labs). Nothing here is lab-validated, and nothing here is medical advice. The Evidence tab shows how often the held-out judge agrees with the steering classifiers, including where it does not.

    Design small molecules

    A diffusion model denoises a molecular latent code into a structure. Three classifiers, trained on noised codes, steer every denoising step toward brain penetration, PTP-family activity and low toxicity. A separate held-out judge, built on different features and trained on different data (for brain penetration and toxicity), scores the result.

    0 = unguided diffusion. Higher pushes harder on the classifiers.

    Design gene-editing guide sequences

    The same engine, a different alphabet. It generates 30-nucleotide SpCas9 guide contexts (4 nt, a 20-nt protospacer, the NGG PAM, 3 nt). A classifier ensemble trained on noised sequences steers toward high cutting efficiency; a held-out logistic model trained on a different lab’s library scores the result. This is the proof of mechanism for sequence design; to target PTPRS itself, use the scanner.
