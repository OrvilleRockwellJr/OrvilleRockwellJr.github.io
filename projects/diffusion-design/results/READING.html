How to read these numbers

Every number below is a mean ± SD over three seeds with identical starting noise across settings (250 molecule latents and 300 guides per seed). The critic is the model that steers; the judge is a separate model on different features, trained on separate data (see the Method tab for what is independent and what is not), that never steers. "Judge pass" means the judge scores the sample above 0.5 on every objective (molecules: brain-penetrant, PTP-active, non-toxic; guides: judge says efficient and the site passes a usability check).

Molecules

  • Guidance without the gate collapses diversity. At γ = 4, unique valid molecules fall from 99.6% (unguided) to 22.9%. With the trust gate, 85.0% stay unique.
  • The gate keeps more of the gain honest. At γ = 4 the critic-judge gap on activity is 0.32 with the gate and 0.57 without. The judge pass rate on all three objectives is 28.7% ± 2.6 with the gate, 13.3% ± 1.6 without, and 7.5% ± 1.3 unguided.
  • Against the filter baseline. Keeping the critic's top 10% of unguided samples gives a judge pass rate of 30.7% ± 10.1. Guided without the gate at γ = 4 and filtered the same way: 5.3% ± 4.6. Guided with the gate: 72.0% ± 8.0. Without the gate the critic's top picks do worse than unguided ones, the pattern reward hacking predicts.
  • Costs. Only about 46% of decoded latents are valid SMILES at γ = 4. The autoencoder reconstructs 81% of held-out ZINC molecules exactly. The three objectives conflict: the judge's P(toxic) falls most (0.50 to 0.41) and its P(active) rises from 0.45 to 0.62.
  • Limit. A ZINC molecule drawn at random passes the judge on all three 18.8% of the time. Unguided generation (7.5%) is worse than that; gated guidance at γ = 4 (28.7%) is better, before any filtering. The activity judge still shares ChEMBL with the activity critic, so that objective is the least independent.

Gene-editing guides

  • Guidance works and is not obviously gamed. Judge P(efficient) rises from 0.47 (unguided) to 0.81 at γ = 16 (0.80 with the gate), and the critic-judge gap stays small (0.06 to 0.07). The judge was trained on a different lab's library than the critic.
  • The gate did not help here. At every γ the ungated runs scored slightly higher on the judge (for example 85% vs 83% passing at γ = 16, within noise). The gate only weakened the push; the critic and judge agree closely on these sites, so there was little for it to correct.
  • Limit. The critic (trained on the Kim 2019 library) scores AUC 0.82 on its own lab's held-out guides, 0.84 on the other lab's library and 0.73 on the Doench genes; the judge (trained on the CRISPRon-2021 library) scores 0.82, 0.87 and 0.75. Filtering unguided guides to the critic's top 10% already passes 92%, so filtering is a strong baseline here. Real held-out guides pass the judge about 40% of the time; generated guides at γ = 16 pass 85%, which says what the judge likes, not what a cell does. Generated guides are not targeted to PTPRS; use the scanner for that.

A reward-hacking example

With an earlier version of the models, a 48-carbon chain with no rings (MW 675, cLogP 19) scored 100% from the brain-penetration and activity critics and passed the held-out judge on all three objectives. Both models had seen only a few thousand labeled molecules and never anything like it. That is the failure the judge is meant to expose, and there it did not: the judge is a fingerprint model, and so it can also be fooled off-distribution. The shortlist therefore applies a basic drug-likeness filter and reports how many candidates it removed. A pass is a prediction, not a property.

Aiming guides at PTPRS

  • Generic guides are not on target. Efficiency-steered guides matched a real PTPRS site 0% of the time, so they were never locus-specific.
  • Locus guidance fixes that. A target-site critic (the exact diffusion score of the library of PTPRS cut sites) makes 100% of generated guides exact PTPRS sites, 92% distinct.
  • But efficiency steering is easily swamped. An untilted library pulls every sample to a random site: success equals a random draw from the library (31% vs 35%). Weighting the library by the efficiency critic ("tilt") raises success to 57% ± 1.
  • Scan-and-rank wins. Simply ranking every PTPRS site by the critic gives 77% success. For a known locus the Scan tab is the better tool; the generator is useful for the integrated molecule-plus-guide workflow and for sequences without an exhaustive site list, not as a replacement for scanning.

What this does and does not show

The trust gate is the useful part for molecules, where critics trained on a few thousand labels meet a generator that wanders off their data. It is not useful for guides in this setup. Neither result involves synthesis, wet-lab or animal data.
