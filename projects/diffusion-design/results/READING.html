How to read these numbers

Every number below is a mean ± SD over three seeds with identical starting noise across settings (250 molecule latents and 300 guides per seed). The critic is the model that steers; the judge is a separate model on different features that never steers. "Judge pass" means the judge scores the sample above 0.5 on every objective (molecules: brain-penetrant, PTP-active, non-toxic; guides: judge says efficient and the site passes a usability check).

Molecules

  • Guidance without the gate collapses diversity. At γ = 4, unique valid molecules fall from 99.6% (unguided) to 28.9%. With the trust gate, 86.4% stay unique.
  • The gate keeps more of the gain honest. At γ = 4 the critic-judge gap on activity is 0.32 with the gate and 0.54 without. The judge pass rate on all three objectives is 34.3% ± 3.6 with the gate, 16.7% ± 4.5 without, and 13.5% ± 1.5 unguided.
  • Against the filter baseline. Keeping the critic's top 10% of unguided samples gives a judge pass rate of 49.3% ± 6.1. Guided with the gate at γ = 4, then filtered the same way: 81.3% ± 6.1.
  • Costs. Only about 46% of decoded latents are valid SMILES at γ = 4. The autoencoder reconstructs 81% of held-out ZINC molecules exactly. The three objectives conflict, so toxicity improves most (judge P(toxic) 0.36 to 0.23) and activity least.
  • Limit. A ZINC molecule drawn at random passes the judge on all three 34.8% of the time, so unguided generation is worse than the training pool on this metric. Gated guidance at γ = 4 only matches that pool before filtering.

Gene-editing guides

  • Guidance works and is not obviously gamed. Judge P(efficient) rises from 0.49 (unguided) to 0.79 at γ = 16, and the critic-judge gap stays small (0.06).
  • The gate did not help here. At every γ the ungated runs scored higher on the judge (for example 0.79 vs 0.75 at γ = 16). The gate only weakened the push; I did not test why, but the critic and judge agree closely on these sites, so there was little for it to correct.
  • Limit. Critic AUC on held-out genes is 0.72 and the judge's is 0.73, so both are modest predictors. Filtering unguided guides to the critic's top 10% already passes 90%, so filtering is a strong baseline here. Real guides from held-out genes pass the judge 43.6% of the time; generated guides at γ = 16 pass 83.3%, but that reflects what the judge learned from the same labels. Generated guides are not targeted to PTPRS; use the scanner for that.

What this does and does not show

The trust gate is the useful part for molecules, where critics trained on a few thousand labels meet a generator that wanders off their data. It is not useful for guides in this setup. Neither result involves synthesis, wet-lab or animal data.
